## 1. Why GEO?

**GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)** is the practice of optimizing web content so that AI-powered search engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Bing Copilot — can discover, understand, and cite your content in their generated answers.

### From Blue Links to Generated Answers

Traditional SEO gets you ranked in a list of blue links. GEO gets you **quoted inside the answer itself**.

The shift is real: when someone asks Perplexity "how to set up a personal website with Hugo and Docker," the AI doesn't show ten links — it synthesizes an answer and cites 2–3 sources. If your site isn't optimized for AI consumption, you're invisible in that answer, even if you rank #1 on Google.

### Why Personal Websites Need GEO Even More

Big publishers have teams tuning their pages for search. A personal website usually has one author and no SEO budget. That's exactly why GEO matters more here:

- **Discovery is harder.** A personal site has fewer backlinks, so it can't out-rank large domains on traditional SEO. Being *cited* by an AI is a shortcut to visibility.
- **The author is the brand.** AI engines weigh expertise and identity (E-E-A-T). A personal site can signal this directly through a `Person` schema — something a faceless corporate blog can't do as cleanly.
- **Niche questions favor first-hand experience.** Queries like "how I deployed Hugo with Docker and Cloudflare" are answered best by someone who actually did it. GEO makes that first-hand content extractable.

In short: GEO is the highest-leverage growth channel for a solo website, because it rewards clear, verifiable, first-hand content over raw domain authority.

## 2. What Should GEO Include? A Checklist

GEO isn't one trick — it's a stack of signals. Here's the complete checklist I work from, grouped into four layers from "let the AI in" to "make the AI trust you."

### Layer 1 — Crawler Access (let AI engines find you)

| Item | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| `robots.txt` | Explicitly allow AI user-agents (GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, etc.) |
| `sitemap.xml` | Give crawlers a complete URL list |
| `llms.txt` | An LLM-friendly site guide: who you are, what's here, key pages |

### Layer 2 — Structured Data (let AI engines understand you)

| Schema type | What it tells the AI |
|-------------|----------------------|
| `Person` | Verifiable author identity + expertise (E-E-A-T) |
| `BlogPosting` | Article title, date, author, description |
| `FAQPage` | Question–answer pairs, ready to quote |
| `HowTo` | Step-by-step procedures |
| `<time datetime>` | Machine-readable freshness signal |

### Layer 3 — Content Structure (let AI engines quote you)

- **Lead with the answer** — a clear, citable statement first, then elaboration
- **Use specifics and statistics** — "95%+ accuracy" beats "high accuracy"
- **Add comparison tables** — structured data the AI can lift directly
- **Tag code blocks with a language** — so snippets are identifiable and citable
- **Use descriptive headings** — "How to Deploy Hugo with Docker," not "Deployment"

### Layer 4 — Authority Signals (let AI engines trust you)

- Consistent author identity across pages
- `sameAs` links to verifiable profiles (GitHub, social)
- `knowsAbout` / expertise areas
- Fresh, dated, first-hand content

Layers 1–2 are mostly one-time technical setup. Layers 3–4 are ongoing editorial habits. You can ship Layer 1 in five minutes and get most of the discovery benefit immediately.

## 3. Case Study: GEO on This Hugo Website

Now let me walk through how I applied that checklist to this site — a Hugo + PaperMod personal website deployed on Cloudflare Workers.

### The Audit

I started by auditing what was already in place:

| Area | Status | GEO Impact |
|------|--------|------------|
| Meta descriptions | Present on all pages | Good — AI uses these for summaries |
| Open Graph / Twitter Cards | Configured | Good — social signals help AI trust |
| JSON-LD (BlogPosting) | Auto-generated by PaperMod | Good — but missing key fields |
| JSON-LD (Organization) | Homepage only | Partial — no Person schema for author |
| Semantic HTML | header, nav, main, article, h1–h4 | Good — AI parsers rely on structure |
| Sitemap.xml | Present | Good — but no robots.txt to guide crawlers |
| robots.txt | **Missing** | Bad — AI crawlers don't know they're welcome |
| llms.txt | **Missing** | Bad — no AI-specific site guide |
| FAQ / HowTo schema | **Missing** | Bad — AI loves Q&A and step-by-step formats |
| `<time>` elements | **Missing** | Bad — dates not machine-readable |
| Author identity | Name only, no Person schema | Weak — AI can't verify expertise |
| Content structure | Narrative-heavy, few direct answers | Weak — AI prefers citable facts upfront |

The traditional-SEO foundation was solid, but the GEO-specific gaps meant AI engines were likely skipping my content or citing it poorly. Here's what I changed, mapped to the checklist layers.

### 3.1 robots.txt for AI Crawlers (Layer 1)

AI search engines use specific user-agents. Without a robots.txt, some crawlers default to caution and skip your site entirely.

I created `static/robots.txt`:

```
User-agent: *
Allow: /

User-agent: GPTBot
Allow: /

User-agent: ChatGPT-User
Allow: /

User-agent: Google-Extended
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

User-agent: ClaudeBot
Allow: /

User-agent: Bytespider
Allow: /

Sitemap: https://blog.wangxiaoyuan.top/sitemap.xml
```

This explicitly welcomes all major AI crawlers and points them to the sitemap.

### 3.2 llms.txt — A Guide for AI Models (Layer 1)

[llms.txt](https://llmstxt.org/) is an emerging standard (like robots.txt, but for LLMs). It tells AI models what your site is about, what content is available, and how to navigate it.

I created `static/llms.txt`:

```markdown
# Rocky's Website

> Personal website of Rocky Wang — Software Engineer & Content Creator
> specializing in software architecture, AI, and digital dentistry.

## About the Author

Rocky Wang is a software engineer and content creator focused on:
- Software architecture and full-stack development
- AI-assisted development workflows (OpenClaw, Whisper, LLMs)
- Digital dentistry technology (CAD/CAM, 3D printing, AI design)
- Self-hosted infrastructure (Docker, Hugo, Cloudflare)

## Content Sections

- [Posts](https://blog.wangxiaoyuan.top/posts/): Technical articles and tutorials
- [Videos](https://blog.wangxiaoyuan.top/videos/): Video demonstrations and webinars
- [About](https://blog.wangxiaoyuan.top/about/): Author bio and social links
- [Booking](https://blog.wangxiaoyuan.top/booking/): Schedule a meeting

## Key Articles

- [Set up my personal site with OpenClaw](https://blog.wangxiaoyuan.top/posts/set-up-my-personal-site-with-openclaw/): Step-by-step tutorial for building a self-hosted Hugo site with Docker and Cloudflare
- [Monitor Digital Dentistry with OpenClaw](https://blog.wangxiaoyuan.top/posts/monitor-digital-dentistry-with-openclaw/): Building an AI-powered RSS monitoring system for dental industry news
- [Let OpenClaw Understand My Voice Instructions](https://blog.wangxiaoyuan.top/posts/let-openclaw-understand-my-voice-instructions/): Integrating local Whisper speech recognition for offline voice-to-text

## Technical Stack

Hugo (static site generator) + PaperMod theme + Docker + Nginx + Cloudflare Workers

## Contact

- GitHub: https://github.com/fightingplane
- Email: juster.wang@gmail.com
```

### 3.3 Person Schema (Layers 2 + 4)

PaperMod generates `BlogPosting` JSON-LD automatically, but the author was just a name string. AI engines need a verifiable `Person` entity to assess expertise and authority (E-E-A-T).

I created a custom partial `layouts/partials/extend_head.html` to inject additional structured data:

```html
{{- if .IsPage }}
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Person",
  "name": "Rocky Wang",
  "url": "https://blog.wangxiaoyuan.top/about/",
  "image": "https://blog.wangxiaoyuan.top/images/avatar.jpg",
  "jobTitle": "Software Engineer",
  "worksFor": {
    "@type": "Organization",
    "name": "Self-employed"
  },
  "sameAs": [
    "https://github.com/fightingplane",
    "https://www.facebook.com/wangxiaoyuan1987"
  ],
  "knowsAbout": [
    "Software Architecture",
    "Artificial Intelligence",
    "Digital Dentistry",
    "Hugo",
    "Docker",
    "Cloudflare"
  ]
}
</script>
{{- end }}
```

### 3.4 FAQ Schema on Tutorial Posts (Layer 2)

AI engines disproportionately cite FAQ-formatted content because it maps directly to question–answer pairs. I added FAQ schema to my tutorial post about setting up the website.

In the post's front matter, I added a `faq` parameter, and in the custom partial:

```html
{{- if .Params.faq }}
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {{- range $i, $q := .Params.faq }}
    {{- if $i }},{{ end }}
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "{{ $q.question }}",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "{{ $q.answer }}"
      }
    }
    {{- end }}
  ]
}
</script>
{{- end }}
```

Then in the post's front matter:

```yaml
faq:
  - question: "What tools do I need to build a personal website with Hugo?"
    answer: "You need Hugo (static site generator), a theme like PaperMod, Docker for containerization, and a domain with Cloudflare for DNS and SSL."
  - question: "How do I deploy a Hugo site with Docker?"
    answer: "Create a multi-stage Dockerfile that builds the Hugo site in the first stage and serves it with Nginx in the second stage. Run the container on port 8081 behind an Nginx reverse proxy."
  - question: "Can I use AI to help build my website?"
    answer: "Yes. AI assistants like OpenClaw can execute commands, create files, configure Docker and Nginx, and manage deployments — reducing setup time from days to hours."
```

### 3.5 HowTo Schema for Step-by-Step Content (Layer 2)

For tutorial posts with numbered steps, HowTo schema tells AI engines exactly what procedure the content describes:

```html
{{- if .Params.howto }}
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "HowTo",
  "name": "{{ .Title }}",
  "description": "{{ .Description }}",
  "step": [
    {{- range $i, $s := .Params.howto }}
    {{- if $i }},{{ end }}
    {
      "@type": "HowToStep",
      "position": {{ add $i 1 }},
      "name": "{{ $s.name }}",
      "text": "{{ $s.text }}"
    }
    {{- end }}
  ]
}
</script>
{{- end }}
```

### 3.6 Content Restructuring (Layer 3)

AI engines prefer content that leads with **direct, citable answers** rather than burying facts in narrative paragraphs. I applied these principles to existing and new articles:

- **Lead with the answer**: Start each section with a clear statement, then elaborate
- **Use statistics and specifics**: "95%+ accuracy" instead of "high accuracy"
- **Add comparison tables**: AI can extract structured comparisons easily
- **Include code blocks with language tags**: AI can identify and cite code snippets
- **Use descriptive headings**: "How to Deploy Hugo with Docker" instead of "Deployment"

### 3.7 Machine-Readable Dates (Layer 2)

PaperMod displays dates as text but doesn't wrap them in `<time>` elements. I overrode the date partial to add proper datetime attributes:

```html
<time datetime="{{ .Date.Format "2006-01-02T15:04:05Z07:00" }}">
  {{ .Date.Format "January 2, 2006" }}
</time>
```

This helps AI engines understand content freshness — a key ranking signal for generative search.

### What This Means in Practice

When someone asks an AI search engine:

> "How do I build a personal website with Hugo and Docker?"

The AI can now:
1. **Discover** the site via robots.txt and sitemap
2. **Understand** the content via llms.txt and structured data
3. **Verify** the author's expertise via Person schema
4. **Extract** a direct answer from FAQ/HowTo schema
5. **Cite** the article with proper attribution and date

Without GEO, the AI might find the page via traditional crawling but would struggle to extract a clean, citable answer — and would likely cite a competitor with better structured data instead.

### Key Takeaways

1. **GEO is not replacing SEO — it's layering on top of it.** All the traditional SEO work (meta tags, semantic HTML, sitemap) still matters. GEO adds AI-specific signals.

2. **robots.txt and llms.txt are the lowest-effort, highest-impact changes.** Five minutes of work, and you've explicitly invited AI crawlers and given them a site map.

3. **Structured data is the bridge between your content and AI understanding.** FAQ and HowTo schemas map directly to how AI engines formulate answers.

4. **Content structure matters more than keywords.** AI engines don't count keyword density — they look for clear, citable statements backed by specifics.

5. **Author identity builds trust.** A Person schema with verifiable social links helps AI engines assess your expertise (E-E-A-T), which influences whether they cite you.
